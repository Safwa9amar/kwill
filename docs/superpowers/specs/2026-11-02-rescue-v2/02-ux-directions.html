Rescue · how it should feel for the student

Three directions for the same 8 stages, drawn in the app's light theme (dark mirrors it) and in Arabic, RTL-first. Each row shows the three moments that decide whether it feels smooth: the free diagnosis, a question, and the wait. Click the direction you want; I'll draw every screen of it next.
One thing at a timeEach screen asks for one decision, with the button under the thumb.
Their thesis stays visibleTheir own pages, not icons, so they can see what is happening.
Reassure, don't judge"47 things we'll fix", not "47 violations". The original is always kept.
Never a blank spinnerProgress is shown on the pages themselves, with a time left.
Leave any timeThe work runs on the server. A push arrives, and reopening resumes where they were.
Plain words"We're building your table of contents", never "section break" or "field".
A

Guided steps

A calm full-screen wizard: a progress bar on top, one decision per screen, a sticky button at the bottom.
+Familiar and the simplest to build
+Works the same for any thesis
−Their thesis is invisible until the end, so it feels like filling in a form
−A question can't show where in the thesis it applies

B

Living thesis recommended

Their own pages fill the top of the screen the whole way through. A sheet below changes with each step, so the student never leaves the screen.
+They watch their own thesis being fixed, which builds trust and is a pleasure to watch
+Each question is shown on the real page, highlighted
+One screen with no navigation jumps, the smoothest of the three
+The "before / after" switch is the sales pitch
−Needs page images at every step. They exist (Kwill's converter), but they have to load fast

C

Inside the chat

Kwill runs the rescue as a conversation. The diagnosis, questions and progress arrive as cards in the thread.
+Fits the chat-first app, and the student can ask anything along the way
+Questions become one-tap chips
−A long rescue gets buried in the thread and is hard to come back to
−Rules, cover choice and payment feel cramped as chat cards
−Mixes "the assistant writes with me" with "a paid service fixes my file"
